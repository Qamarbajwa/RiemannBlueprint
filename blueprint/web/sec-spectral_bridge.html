0.7 The Spectral Bridge: Chebyshev’s \(\psi (x)\) and Riemann’s Explicit Formula

In the preceding sections, we observed that while additive partitions of integers fail inside the critical strip due to conditional convergence traps, the multiplicative structure of primes and prime powers can be concentrated into a single arithmetic spectral measure: the von Mangoldt function \(\Lambda (n)\). In this section, we construct the exact spectral bridge connecting the distribution of primes to the non-trivial zeros \(\rho \) of \(\zeta (s)\), deriving Riemann’s Explicit Formula and establishing the Spectral Stability Theorem.

0.7.1 Chebyshev’s Functions and the Logarithmic Derivative

Definition 0.7.1 Chebyshev Functions
✓
#

  • RiemannBlueprint.Section7_SpectralBridge.chebyshevPsi

For \(x \ge 1\), Chebyshev’s theta and psi functions are defined by:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:chebyshev_defs} \theta (x) \coloneqq \sum _{p \le x} \ln p, \qquad \psi (x) \coloneqq \sum _{n \le x} \Lambda (n) = \sum _{p^k \le x} \ln p. \end{equation}
77

Notice that \(\psi (x)\) decomposes naturally across the prime and composite sets:

\begin{equation} \psi (x) = \theta (x) + \sum _{k=2}^{\lfloor \log _2 x \rfloor } \theta \left(x^{1/k}\right). \end{equation}
78

The first term \(\theta (x)\) is the pure prime contribution, while the second term captures the exact contribution of the pure prime powers \(\mathbb {C}_{\mathrm{pure}}\). Mixed composites \(\mathbb {C}_{\mathrm{mixed}}\) have \(\Lambda (c) = 0\) and contribute nothing.

Lemma 0.7.2 Logarithmic Derivative of the Riemann Zeta Function
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 0.7.1

For every \(s \in \mathbb {C}\) with \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\), the logarithmic derivative of \(\zeta (s)\) is given by the Dirichlet series:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:zeta_log_derivative} -\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} = \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda (n)}{n^s}. \end{equation}
79

Proof ▶

For \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\), the Euler product converges absolutely:

\[ \zeta (s) = \prod _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \left(1 - p^{-s}\right)^{-1}. \]

Taking the principal branch of the complex logarithm:

\[ \ln \zeta (s) = -\sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \ln \left(1 - p^{-s}\right). \]

Differentiating both sides with respect to \(s\):

\[ \frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s} \ln \zeta (s) = -\sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s} \ln \left(1 - p^{-s}\right) = -\sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \frac{p^{-s} \ln p}{1 - p^{-s}}. \]

Multiplying by \(-1\) and expanding the geometric series \(\frac{1}{1 - p^{-s}} = \sum _{m=0}^\infty p^{-m s}\) (valid because \(|p^{-s}| = p^{-\sigma } {\lt} 1\) for \(\sigma {\gt} 1\)):

\[ -\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} = \sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \sum _{k=1}^\infty \frac{\ln p}{p^{k s}}. \]

By the definition of the von Mangoldt function \(\Lambda (n)\) (Proposition 0.3.4), \(\Lambda (n) = \ln p\) if \(n = p^k\), and \(\Lambda (n) = 0\) otherwise. Re-indexing the double sum over all positive integers \(n \in \mathbb {N}\):

\[ -\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} = \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda (n)}{n^s}. \]

Absolute convergence follows from \(\sum \Lambda (n) n^{-\sigma } = -\zeta '(\sigma )/\zeta (\sigma ) {\lt} \infty \) for \(\sigma {\gt} 1\).

Theorem 0.7.4 Riemann’s Explicit Formula
✓
#

Uses

  • Lemma 0.7.2

For every \(x {\gt} 1\):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:riemann_explicit_formula} \psi _0(x) = x - \sum _{\rho } \frac{x^\rho }{\rho } - \ln (2\pi ) - \frac{1}{2}\ln \left(1 - x^{-2}\right), \end{equation}
83

where the sum over \(\rho \) runs over all non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta (s)\) in the critical strip \(0 {\lt} \operatorname {Re}(\rho ) {\lt} 1\), paired symmetrically as:

\begin{equation} \sum _{\rho } \frac{x^\rho }{\rho } \coloneqq \lim _{T \to \infty } \sum _{|\operatorname {Im}(\rho )| \le T} \frac{x^\rho }{\rho }. \end{equation}
84

Proof ▶

Let \(c = 1 + \epsilon {\gt} 1\). By Perron’s formula:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:perron_psi} \psi _0(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \lim _{T \to \infty } \int _{c - i T}^{c + i T} \left( -\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} \right) \frac{x^s}{s} \, \mathrm{d}s. \end{equation}
85

We consider a closed rectangular contour \(\mathcal{C}_{T, U}\) with vertices:

\[ c - i T, \quad c + i T, \quad -U + i T, \quad -U - i T, \]

where \(U = 2K + 1\) (\(K \in \mathbb {N}\)) is an odd integer chosen to pass midway between the trivial zeros at \(s = -2m\), and \(T\) is chosen in the range \([T_0, T_0 + 1]\) such that \(|T - \gamma _n| \gg \frac{1}{\ln T}\) for all ordinates \(\gamma _n\) of non-trivial zeros (avoiding singularities).

By Cauchy’s Residue Theorem:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:cauchy_psi_integral} \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint _{\mathcal{C}_{T, U}} \left( -\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} \right) \frac{x^s}{s} \, \mathrm{d}s = \sum _{s_0 \in \operatorname {Int}(\mathcal{C}_{T, U})} \operatorname {Res}_{s = s_0} \left[ \left(-\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)}\right) \frac{x^s}{s} \right]. \end{equation}
86

We evaluate the residues of the integrand \(F(s) \coloneqq \left(-\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)}\right) \frac{x^s}{s}\) at all its poles inside the contour:

  1. The Pole at \(s = 1\): Riemann showed that \(\zeta (s)\) has a simple pole at \(s = 1\) with residue \(1\). Near \(s = 1\):

    \[ \zeta (s) = \frac{1}{s-1} + \mathcal{O}(1) \implies \zeta '(s) = -\frac{1}{(s-1)^2} + \mathcal{O}(1) \implies -\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} = \frac{1}{s-1} + \mathcal{O}(1). \]

    Thus \(-\zeta '/\zeta \) has a simple pole at \(s = 1\) with residue \(+1\). Since \(x^s / s\) is holomorphic at \(s = 1\):

    \[ \operatorname {Res}_{s=1} F(s) = \lim _{s \to 1} (s-1) \left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right) \frac{x^s}{s} = \frac{x^1}{1} = x. \]

  2. The Non-Trivial Zeros \(s = \rho \): Let \(\rho = \beta + i \gamma \) be a non-trivial zero of \(\zeta (s)\) with multiplicity \(m_\rho \ge 1\). Near \(s = \rho \):

    \[ \zeta (s) = (s - \rho )^{m_\rho } g(s) \quad \text{with } g(\rho ) \neq 0. \]

    Differentiating:

    \[ \frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} = \frac{m_\rho }{s - \rho } + \frac{g'(s)}{g(s)} \implies -\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} = -\frac{m_\rho }{s - \rho } + \mathcal{O}(1). \]

    Therefore, the residue of \(-\zeta '/\zeta \) at \(s = \rho \) is \(-m_\rho \). Assuming simplicity of zeros (\(m_\rho = 1\)):

    \[ \operatorname {Res}_{s=\rho } F(s) = - \frac{x^\rho }{\rho }. \]

  3. The Pole at \(s = 0\): At \(s = 0\), \(x^s / s\) has a simple pole with residue \(x^0 = 1\). The factor \(-\zeta '(s)/\zeta (s)\) is analytic at \(s = 0\). From the functional equation \(\zeta (s) = 2^s \pi ^{s-1} \sin (\pi s / 2) \Gamma (1-s) \zeta (1-s)\), Taylor expanding about \(s = 0\) yields:

    \[ \zeta (0) = -\frac{1}{2}, \qquad \zeta '(0) = -\frac{1}{2}\ln (2\pi ). \]

    Therefore:

    \[ \operatorname {Res}_{s=0} F(s) = -\frac{\zeta '(0)}{\zeta (0)} \cdot x^0 = -\frac{-\frac{1}{2}\ln (2\pi )}{-\frac{1}{2}} = -\ln (2\pi ). \]

  4. The Trivial Zeros \(s = -2m\) (\(m \in \mathbb {N}\)): The trivial zeros of \(\zeta (s)\) are simple zeros at \(s = -2, -4, -6, \dots \). At each \(s = -2m\), \(-\zeta '/\zeta \) has residue \(-1\). Thus:

    \[ \operatorname {Res}_{s = -2m} F(s) = - \frac{x^{-2m}}{-2m} = \frac{x^{-2m}}{2m}. \]

    Summing these residues over all \(m \ge 1\) inside the contour:

    \[ \sum _{m=1}^\infty \frac{x^{-2m}}{2m} = \frac{1}{2} \sum _{m=1}^\infty \frac{(x^{-2})^m}{m}. \]

    Recalling the Taylor series expansion for the natural logarithm: \(\sum _{m=1}^\infty \frac{u^m}{m} = -\ln (1 - u)\) for \(|u| {\lt} 1\). Setting \(u = x^{-2}\) (which satisfies \(|x^{-2}| {\lt} 1\) since \(x {\gt} 1\)):

    \[ \sum _{m=1}^\infty \frac{x^{-2m}}{2m} = -\frac{1}{2}\ln \left(1 - x^{-2}\right). \]

Now we estimate the contour integrals along the three non-essential segments:

  • Along the horizontal segments \([-U \pm i T, c \pm i T]\): By standard bounds on \(\zeta '/\zeta \) (see Davenport  [ 4 ] , Chapter 17), \(\left|\frac{\zeta '(\sigma \pm i T)}{\zeta (\sigma \pm i T)}\right| = \mathcal{O}(\ln ^2 T)\) for \(\sigma \in [-U, c]\). The integral is bounded by \(\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x^c \ln ^2 T}{T \ln x}\right) \to 0\) as \(T \to \infty \).

  • Along the left vertical segment \([-U - i T, -U + i T]\): The integrand decays as \(x^{-U}\). As \(U \to \infty \), \(x^{-U} \to 0\) since \(x {\gt} 1\).

Taking the limits \(U \to \infty \) and \(T \to \infty \), the contour integral in ?? reduces to the Perron integral ??. Equating the Perron integral with the sum of all residues yields:

\[ \psi _0(x) = x - \sum _{\rho } \frac{x^\rho }{\rho } - \ln (2\pi ) - \frac{1}{2}\ln \left(1 - x^{-2}\right). \]

This completes the formal proof.

0.7.3 The Spectral Stability Theorem

Riemann’s Explicit Formula reveals that the primes are not scattered randomly, but oscillate around their mean density \(x\) according to the Fourier frequencies \(\gamma = \operatorname {Im}(\rho )\) of the non-trivial zeros:

Theorem 0.7.5 Spectral Stability Theorem
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 0.7.4

Lean declarations

  • RiemannBlueprint.Section7_SpectralBridge.spectral_stability_theorem

Let \(\Theta \coloneqq \sup \{ \operatorname {Re}(\rho ) : \zeta (\rho ) = 0, \; 0 {\lt} \operatorname {Re}(\rho ) {\lt} 1 \} \). Then:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:psi_error_bound} \psi (x) - x = \mathcal{O}\left( x^\Theta \ln ^2 x \right) \quad \text{as } x \to \infty . \end{equation}
87

In particular, the Riemann Hypothesis (\(\Theta = \frac{1}{2}\)) holds if and only if:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:psi_rh_bound} \psi (x) = x + \mathcal{O}\left( x^{1/2} \ln ^2 x \right). \end{equation}
88

Furthermore, this is equivalent to Helge von Koch’s theorem (1901) for the prime counting function:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:pi_von_koch} \pi (x) = \operatorname {Li}(x) + \mathcal{O}\left( x^{1/2} \ln x \right). \end{equation}
89

Proof ▶

From Riemann’s Explicit Formula ??:

\[ \psi (x) - x = -\sum _{|\gamma | \le T} \frac{x^\rho }{\rho } + \mathcal{O}\left( \frac{x \ln ^2 x}{T} + \ln x \right). \]

For each non-trivial zero \(\rho = \beta + i \gamma \):

\[ \left| \frac{x^\rho }{\rho } \right| = \frac{|x^{\beta + i \gamma }|}{|\beta + i \gamma |} = \frac{x^\beta }{\sqrt{\beta ^2 + \gamma ^2}} \le \frac{x^\Theta }{|\gamma |}. \]

Summing over all \(|\gamma | \le T\):

\[ \left| \sum _{|\gamma | \le T} \frac{x^\rho }{\rho } \right| \le x^\Theta \sum _{|\gamma | \le T} \frac{1}{|\gamma |}. \]

By Riemann’s zero-counting formula \(N(T) = \frac{T}{2\pi }\ln \frac{T}{2\pi } - \frac{T}{2\pi } + \mathcal{O}(\ln T)\):

\[ \sum _{|\gamma | \le T} \frac{1}{|\gamma |} = \int _0^T \frac{\mathrm{d}N(t)}{t} = \left[ \frac{N(t)}{t} \right]_0^T + \int _0^T \frac{N(t)}{t^2} \, \mathrm{d}t = \mathcal{O}\left( \ln ^2 T \right). \]

Therefore:

\[ \psi (x) - x = \mathcal{O}\left( x^\Theta \ln ^2 T + \frac{x \ln ^2 x}{T} \right). \]

Choosing \(T = x^{1 - \Theta + \epsilon }\) (or \(T = x\) when \(\Theta = 1/2\)) balances the terms, yielding:

\[ \psi (x) - x = \mathcal{O}\left( x^\Theta \ln ^2 x \right). \]

If \(\Theta = 1/2\), the error is \(\mathcal{O}(x^{1/2} \ln ^2 x)\). Conversely, if \(\psi (x) - x = \mathcal{O}(x^{\Theta _0})\) for some \(\Theta _0 {\lt} 1\), then by Mellin inversion \(-\frac{\zeta '(s)}{\zeta (s)} - \frac{s}{s-1} = s \int _1^\infty \frac{\psi (x) - x}{x^{s+1}} \, \mathrm{d}x\) converges and is holomorphic for \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} \Theta _0\). Hence \(\zeta (s)\) can have no zeros with \(\operatorname {Re}(\rho ) {\gt} \Theta _0\), which forces \(\Theta \le \Theta _0\). Finally, converting \(\psi (x)\) to \(\pi (x)\) via Riemann–Stieltjes integration:

\[ \pi (x) = \int _{2^-}^x \frac{\mathrm{d}\theta (u)}{\ln u} = \frac{\theta (x)}{\ln x} + \int _2^x \frac{\theta (u)}{u \ln ^2 u} \, \mathrm{d}u. \]

Since \(\psi (x) - \theta (x) = \mathcal{O}(x^{1/2})\), substituting \(\psi (x) = x + \mathcal{O}(x^{1/2}\ln ^2 x)\) yields von Koch’s formula ??.

Remark 0.7.6 Harmonic Interpretation of the Non-Trivial Zeros
#

Theorem 0.7.5 demonstrates that the non-trivial zeros \(\rho = \beta + i \gamma \) are the harmonics of the prime spectrum. The real part \(\beta \) dictates the growth envelope of the wave (\(x^\beta \)), while the imaginary part \(\gamma \) dictates the frequency of oscillation (\(e^{i \gamma \ln x}\)). If any zero existed with \(\beta {\gt} 1/2\), it would generate an amplified ripple \(x^\beta \gg x^{1/2}\), violating the optimal square-root error bound \(\psi (x) - x = \mathcal{O}(x^{1/2}\ln ^2 x)\) equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis. Under RH (\(\beta = 1/2\) for all non-trivial zeros), every oscillatory mode is confined to the critical \(\sqrt{x}\) amplitude envelope (maximal spectral resonance stability).
