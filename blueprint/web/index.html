Additive-Multiplicative Completeness of Prime and Composite Dirichlet Systems: Structural Partitions, Natural Boundaries, and Spectral Equivalences of the Riemann Hypothesis

Qamar Munawer

We formulate an exact arithmetic and analytic framework for the Riemann zeta function \(\zeta (s)\) based on the fundamental partition of the positive integers into three mutually exclusive structural classes: the multiplicative unit \(\{ 1\} \), the prime atoms \(\mathbb {P}\), and the composite lattice \(\mathbb {C}\). We investigate how additive decompositions and multiplicative convolutions over these sets behave across the critical abscissa of convergence \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 1\).

In the half-plane \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\), we establish the exact additive split \(\zeta (s) = 1 + P(s) + \zeta _C(s)\), where \(P(s)\) is the Prime Zeta Function and \(\zeta _C(s)\) is the unweighted Composite Zeta Function, and evaluate the certified 50/50 critical parity exponent \(s^* \approx 1.446537622...\) where prime and composite Dirichlet energies balance. We construct the divisor-weighted composite series \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s) = (\zeta (s)-1)^2 = \sum _{c \in \mathbb {C}} \frac{d(c)-2}{c^s}\), which isolates composites by algebraically annihilating all prime and unit terms. We prove that while \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s)\) extends meromorphically to all of \(\mathbb {C}\) with a single double pole at \(s=1\), the unweighted additive function \(\zeta _C(s)\) inherits a dense natural boundary along the imaginary axis \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\) formed by logarithmic branch-point accumulations.

We conduct a rigorous functional-analytic audit of the \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 1\) boundary, demonstrating why additive and multiplicative completeness arguments cannot be extended into the critical strip \(0 {\lt} \operatorname {Re}(s) {\lt} 1\) without confronting the divergence of coordinate series, the failure of point-wise Euler product convergence, and the Riemann Series Rearrangement Theorem. Finally, we establish the bridge connecting the prime-composite arithmetic system to the non-trivial zeros \(\rho = \beta + i\gamma \) through the reciprocal Euler product via the Mertens equivalence, and through Chebyshev’s \(\psi (x)\) via Perron’s formula and Riemann’s Explicit Formula, proving that the horizontal spectral stability of the explicit oscillatory modes is strictly equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis.
