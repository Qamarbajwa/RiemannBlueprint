Definition 0.2.1Primes, Units, and Divisibility

Let \(a, b \in \mathbb {Z}\). We say that \(a\) divides \(b\), written \(a \mid b\), if there exists an integer \(c \in \mathbb {Z}\) such that \(b = a \cdot c\). An element \(u \in \mathbb {Z}\) is a unit if \(u \mid 1\), which in \(\mathbb {Z}\) implies \(u \in \{ -1, +1\} \). An integer \(p \in \mathbb {Z}\setminus \{ -1, 0, 1\} \) is called prime if its only divisors are \(\pm 1\) and \(\pm p\). Throughout this monograph, \(\mathbb {P}\) denotes the positive primes:

\begin{equation} \mathbb {P}\equiv \{ n \in \mathbb {N}\mid n \ge 2 \text{ and } (d \mid n \implies d \in \{ 1, n\} )\} . \end{equation}
6

Two integers \(a, b \in \mathbb {Z}\) are called coprime (or relatively prime), denoted \(\gcd (a, b) = 1\), if their only common positive divisor is \(1\).

Theorem 0.6.2The Three Analytic Obstructions at \(\operatorname {Re}(s) \le 1\)

Any attempt to extend the additive-multiplicative completeness of \(\zeta (s) = 1 + P(s) + \zeta _C(s)\) point-wise into the critical strip \(0 {\lt} \operatorname {Re}(s) \le 1\) fails due to the following three structural barriers:

  1. Obstruction 1 (Divergence of Coordinate Series): For every \(\sigma \in (0, 1]\), the Prime Zeta series and the Composite Zeta series diverge identically:

    \begin{equation} \sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} p^{-\sigma } = \infty \quad \text{and} \quad \sum _{c \in \mathbb {C}_{\mathrm{comp}}} c^{-\sigma } = \infty . \end{equation}
    69

    Consequently, the sum \(1 + P(s) + \zeta _C(s)\) degenerates into the undefined indeterminate form \(\infty - \infty \).

  2. Obstruction 2 (Failure of Pointwise Euler Product Convergence): For any \(s = \sigma + i t\) with \(0 {\lt} \sigma {\lt} 1\), the partial Euler products \(\Pi _X(s) = \prod _{p \le X} (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}\) do not converge point-wise as \(X \to \infty \). They oscillate indefinitely and fail to define \(\zeta (s)\) point-by-point.

  3. Obstruction 3 (The Riemann Series Rearrangement Violation): For \(0 {\lt} \sigma \le 1\), the series \(\sum n^{-s}\) is not absolutely convergent (\(\sum n^{-\sigma } = \infty \)). By the Riemann Series Theorem, conditionally convergent series cannot be arbitrarily partitioned or rearranged into primes, composites, or modular sub-lattices without altering the sum or inducing divergence.
