\section{The Real-World Picture: A Vibrating Organ Pipe}

Imagine a hollow metal organ pipe of length $1$~meter, and suppose you want to build a \textbf{pure flat tone} (a flat $1$ across the whole pipe) using only \textbf{sawtooth echoes} whose wavelengths are fractions of a meter: $1/1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, \dots, 1/N$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Prime numbers ($2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \dots$)} are the \textbf{fundamental strings}---brand-new, irreducible frequencies entering the discrete clock $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ for the first time.
  \item \textbf{Composite numbers ($4, 6, 8, 9, 10, \dots$)} are \textbf{overtone echoes} on the clock where earlier rhythms collide (for example, $6 = 2 \times 3$ is the first clock tick where $p=2$ and $p=3$ meet).
  \item In the matrices, $\mathbf{D}_{M,N}$ records which earlier rhythms divide clock tick $n$, while $\Sigma_N = Q_{CC} - Q_{CP}Q_{PP}^{-1}Q_{PC}$ tares out the prime dimensions ($p$) from the composite clock ticks ($n$).
\end{itemize}
The \textbf{Riemann Hypothesis} asks whether adding more and more sawtooth echoes ($N \to \infty$) lets us cancel every ripple and drive the leftover sound energy all the way to zero.

\section{Why the Clay Continuity Limit ($N \to \infty$) Is Still Open}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Decreasing and positive does not force limit zero:} Although $E_{\mathrm{dir}}(N+1) \le E_{\mathrm{dir}}(N)$ and prime jumps keep $E_{\mathrm{dir}}(N) > 0$, a decreasing sequence can plateau at $L_\infty > 0$. By Half-Law~3 (\texttt{unitDefectMatrix\_offline\_zero\_floor\_unconditional}), even a single zero $\zeta(s_0)=0$ with $\operatorname{Re}(s_0) > 1/2$ forces $E_{\mathrm{dir}}(N) \ge (2\operatorname{Re}(s_0)-1)/\|s_0\|^2 > 0$ for all $N$.
  \item \textbf{Active-window vs.\ tail trade-off:} At $N=32$, $0\%$ of the energy sits on $(1/2,1]$, $51.05\%$ leaks in $[2,32]$, $12.75\%$ on primes $p > 32$, and $35.41\%$ on composites $n > 32$. Exact inversion $\mathbf{F}_N^{-1}=\mathbf{M}_\mu\mathbf{\Delta}_N$ zeroes $[2,32]$ but blows up the tail unless M\"obius sums have square-root cancellation---already equivalent to RH.
  \item \textbf{Bessel's inequality gives $\le 1$, not $= 1$:} $E_{\mathrm{free}}(N) = 1 - \sum_{k=3}^N \rho_k^2/s_k \ge 0$ guarantees $\sum_{k=3}^\infty \rho_k^2/s_k \le 1$; proving the limit equals $1$ (or $\ln(N_k)E_{\mathrm{dir}}(N_k) \to C_{\mathrm{BBLR}}$) \emph{is} the open Clay Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}
