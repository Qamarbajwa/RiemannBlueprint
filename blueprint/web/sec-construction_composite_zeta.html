0.4 Construction of the Composite Zeta Functions (\(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\))

We now construct the Dirichlet generating functions for the composite system \(\mathbb {C}\) for \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\) using three distinct mathematical formulations.

0.4.1 Method 1: Additive Partition and the 50/50 Equilibrium

Definition 0.4.1 Unweighted Composite Zeta Function
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • RiemannBlueprint.Section4_CompositeZeta.PrimeZeta

For \(s = \sigma + i t\) with \(\sigma {\gt} 1\), the Prime Zeta Function \(P(s)\) and the Composite Zeta Function \(\zeta _C(s)\) are defined by:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:P_and_zetaC} P(s) \equiv \sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \frac{1}{p^s}, \qquad \zeta _C(s) \equiv \sum _{c \in \mathbb {C}} \frac{1}{c^s}. \end{equation}
45

Theorem 0.4.2 Exact Additive Decomposition Identity
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 0.4.1

Lean declarations

  • RiemannBlueprint.Section4_CompositeZeta.additive_identity_relation

For all \(s \in \mathbb {C}\) with \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\), the Riemann zeta function decomposes into the exact three-term identity:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:zeta_additive_identity} \zeta (s) = 1 + P(s) + \zeta _C(s). \end{equation}
46

Equivalently:

\begin{equation} \zeta _C(s) = \zeta (s) - 1 - P(s). \end{equation}
47

Proof ▶

By the arithmetic partition \(\mathbb {N}= \{ 1\} \; \dot{\cup }\; \mathbb {P}\; \dot{\cup }\; \mathbb {C}\) (Eq. ??):

\[ \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s} = \frac{1}{1^s} + \sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \frac{1}{p^s} + \sum _{c \in \mathbb {C}} \frac{1}{c^s}. \]

For \(\sigma {\gt} 1\), the Dirichlet series \(\sum _{n=1}^\infty n^{-\sigma }\) converges absolutely. Since subseries of an absolutely convergent series converge absolutely to the sum of their partitions, \(\zeta (s) = 1 + P(s) + \zeta _C(s)\).

Example 0.4.3 Evaluation at the Basel Value \(s = 2\)
#

At \(s = 2\), \(\zeta (2) = \sum _{n=1}^\infty n^{-2} = \frac{\pi ^2}{6} \approx 1.644934066848\). The Prime Zeta value at \(s = 2\) is:

\[ P(2) = \sum _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \frac{1}{p^2} \approx 0.452247420041. \]

Therefore, the Composite Zeta Function at \(s = 2\) evaluates in closed form to:

\begin{equation} \zeta _C(2) = \frac{\pi ^2}{6} - 1 - P(2) \approx 1.644934066848 - 1.0 - 0.452247420041 = \mathbf{0.192686646807}. \end{equation}
48

Relative to the non-unit sum \(\zeta (2) - 1 \approx 0.644934\):

\[ \text{Prime Share: } \frac{P(2)}{\zeta (2) - 1} \approx 70.12\% , \qquad \text{Composite Share: } \frac{\zeta _C(2)}{\zeta (2) - 1} \approx 29.88\% . \]

0.4.2 Method 2: Local Prime-Power Factors and the Möbius-Logarithmic Bridge

Definition 0.4.4 Local Prime Euler Factor Decomposition
✓
#

For each prime \(p \in \mathbb {P}\) and \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\), the local Euler factor decomposes additively into:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:local_factor} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^s}\right)^{-1} = \sum _{k=0}^\infty \frac{1}{p^{k s}} = 1 + \frac{1}{p^s} + \sum _{k=2}^\infty \frac{1}{p^{k s}} = 1 + \frac{1}{p^s} + \zeta _{\mathrm{pure}, p}(s), \end{equation}
49

where:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:pure_prime_factor} \zeta _{\mathrm{pure}, p}(s) \equiv \sum _{k=2}^\infty \frac{1}{p^{k s}} = \frac{p^{-2s}}{1 - p^{-s}} = \frac{1}{p^s(p^s - 1)} \end{equation}
50

is the local pure composite factor generating all pure powers of \(p\) (\(\{ p^2, p^3, p^4, \dots \} \subset \mathbb {C}_{\mathrm{pure}}\)).

0.4.3 Method 3: Divisor-Weighted Composite Dirichlet Series

While Methods 1 and 2 define unweighted sums over \(\mathbb {C}\), multiplicative arithmetic generates natural divisor-weighted series through Dirichlet convolutions.

Theorem 0.4.6 Divisor-Weighted Composite Series
✓
#

Uses

  • Corollary 0.3.7

Lean declarations

  • RiemannBlueprint.Section4_CompositeZeta.divisor_weight_primes_and_composites

For all \(s \in \mathbb {C}\) with \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\), the shifted square of the Riemann zeta function annihilates the unit \(1\) and every prime \(p \in \mathbb {P}\), generating a pure Dirichlet series supported strictly on the composite numbers \(\mathbb {C}\):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:weighted_composite_series} Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s) \equiv (\zeta (s) - 1)^2 = \sum _{c \in \mathbb {C}} \frac{d(c) - 2}{c^s}, \end{equation}
52

where \(d(c) = \sum _{d \mid c} 1\) is the divisor function.

Proof ▶

Recall that the Dirichlet convolution of \(\zeta (s)\) with itself generates the divisor function:

\[ \zeta (s)^2 = \left( \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s} \right)\left( \sum _{m=1}^\infty \frac{1}{m^s} \right) = \sum _{n=1}^\infty \sum _{m=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(n m)^s} = \sum _{k=1}^\infty \frac{d(k)}{k^s} \quad (\sigma {\gt} 1). \]

Expand the binomial square \((\zeta (s) - 1)^2\):

\[ (\zeta (s) - 1)^2 = \zeta (s)^2 - 2 \zeta (s) + 1. \]

Substitute the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta (s)^2\) and \(\zeta (s)\):

\[ (\zeta (s) - 1)^2 = \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{d(n)}{n^s} - 2 \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s} + 1 = 1 + \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{d(n) - 2}{n^s}. \]

Evaluate the \(n = 1\) term: since \(d(1) = 1\), the \(n = 1\) term is:

\[ \frac{d(1) - 2}{1^s} = \frac{1 - 2}{1} = -1. \]

Adding \(1\) exactly cancels the \(n = 1\) term:

\[ 1 + \frac{d(1) - 2}{1^s} = 1 - 1 = 0. \]

Therefore, the summation begins at \(n = 2\):

\[ (\zeta (s) - 1)^2 = \sum _{n=2}^\infty \frac{d(n) - 2}{n^s}. \]

Now evaluate the numerator coefficient \(d(n) - 2\) across the arithmetic partition \(\mathbb {N}\setminus \{ 1\} = \mathbb {P}\; \dot{\cup }\; \mathbb {C}\):

  1. Primes (\(n = p \in \mathbb {P}\)): By Corollary 0.3.7(b), every prime has exactly two divisors (\(1\) and \(p\)), so \(d(p) = 2\). Therefore:

    \[ d(p) - 2 = 2 - 2 = 0 \quad (\forall p \in \mathbb {P}). \]

    Every prime term in the series vanishes identically!

  2. Composites (\(n = c \in \mathbb {C}\)): By Corollary 0.3.7(c), every composite number has \(d(c) \ge 3\). Therefore:

    \[ d(c) - 2 \ge 3 - 2 = 1 {\gt} 0 \quad (\forall c \in \mathbb {C}). \]

Since the coefficients vanish for \(n = 1\) and for all \(n \in \mathbb {P}\), the non-zero terms are supported strictly on \(n \in \mathbb {C}\):

\[ (\zeta (s) - 1)^2 = \sum _{c \in \mathbb {C}} \frac{d(c) - 2}{c^s}. \]

This completes the proof.

0.4.4 Analytic Continuation vs. Natural Boundary Theorem

A central theorem of this monograph contrasts the global analytic continuation of the weighted composite series against the unweighted additive split.

Theorem 0.4.8 Meromorphic Continuation vs. Natural Boundary
✓
#

Uses

  • Theorem 0.4.6

Lean declarations

  • RiemannBlueprint.Section4_CompositeZeta.analytic_discrepancy_theorem

The analytic continuations of the weighted and unweighted composite generating functions exhibit fundamentally different global structures:

  1. Global Meromorphic Continuation of \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s)\): The divisor-weighted composite series \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s) = (\zeta (s) - 1)^2\) extends meromorphically to the entire complex plane \(\mathbb {C}\). It possesses:

    • A single double pole at \(s = 1\) with Laurent expansion:

      \begin{equation} Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s) = \frac{1}{(s-1)^2} + \frac{2(\gamma - 1)}{s-1} + \mathcal{O}(1) \quad (s \to 1). \end{equation}
      53

    • Double zeros at the trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function \(s = -2m\) for \(m \in \mathbb {N}\):

      \[ Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(-2m) = (\zeta (-2m) - 1)^2 = (0 - 1)^2 = 1 \neq 0, \]

      and zeros wherever \(\zeta (s) = 1\).

    • No natural boundary anywhere in \(\mathbb {C}\).

  2. Natural Boundary of \(\zeta _C(s)\) and \(P(s)\) at \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\): The unweighted Composite Zeta Function \(\zeta _C(s) = \zeta (s) - 1 - P(s)\) cannot be analytically continued past the imaginary axis \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\). The line \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\) is a dense natural boundary formed by the accumulation of logarithmic branch-point singularities.

Proof ▶

Proof of (a): Riemann proved that \(\zeta (s)\) extends meromorphically to all of \(\mathbb {C}\) with its sole singularity being a simple pole at \(s = 1\) with residue \(1\):

\[ \zeta (s) = \frac{1}{s-1} + \gamma + \gamma _1(s-1) + \dots \]

Subtracting \(1\):

\[ \zeta (s) - 1 = \frac{1}{s-1} + (\gamma - 1) + \gamma _1(s-1) + \dots \]

Squaring this expression:

\[ Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s) = (\zeta (s) - 1)^2 = \frac{1}{(s-1)^2} + \frac{2(\gamma - 1)}{s-1} + \mathcal{O}(1). \]

Since \(\zeta (s) - 1\) is holomorphic on \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \{ 1\} \), its square \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s)\) is holomorphic on \(\mathbb {C}\setminus \{ 1\} \) with a single pole of order \(2\) at \(s = 1\). It has no other singularities and therefore no natural boundary.

Proof of (b): By Theorem 0.4.5:

\[ \zeta _C(s) = \zeta (s) - 1 - \sum _{k=1}^\infty \frac{\mu (k)}{k} \ln \zeta (k s). \]

The singularities of \(\zeta _C(s)\) are governed by the singularities of \(P(s) = \sum _{k=1}^\infty \frac{\mu (k)}{k} \ln \zeta (k s)\). The function \(\ln \zeta (w)\) possesses logarithmic branch points at:

  1. Every pole of \(\zeta (w)\), which occurs uniquely at \(w = 1\).

  2. Every zero of \(\zeta (w)\), which occurs at the trivial zeros \(w = -2m\) (\(m \in \mathbb {N}\)) and the non-trivial zeros \(w = \rho _n = \beta _n + i \gamma _n\) with \(\beta _n \in (0, 1)\).

Setting \(w = k s\), we find that \(\ln \zeta (k s)\) has branch points at:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:branch_points} s = \frac{1}{k} \quad (k \in \mathbb {N}), \qquad s = \frac{\rho _n}{k} = \frac{\beta _n + i \gamma _n}{k} \quad (k \in \mathbb {N}, \; n \in \mathbb {Z}\setminus \{ 0\} ). \end{equation}
54

Now consider the limit as \(k \to \infty \):

  • The harmonic pole pre-images \(s_k = 1/k\) form a sequence of real branch points accumulating at \(s = 0\):

    \[ \lim _{k \to \infty } \frac{1}{k} = 0. \]

  • For each non-trivial zero \(\rho _n = \beta _n + i \gamma _n\), the sequence of branch points \(s_{n, k} = \frac{\beta _n + i \gamma _n}{k}\) has real part \(\operatorname {Re}(s_{n, k}) = \frac{\beta _n}{k}\). Since \(\beta _n \in (0, 1)\):

    \[ \lim _{k \to \infty } \operatorname {Re}(s_{n, k}) = \lim _{k \to \infty } \frac{\beta _n}{k} = 0. \]

    Because the imaginary parts \(\gamma _n\) of the non-trivial zeros are unbounded (there are infinitely many zeros, with \(N(T) \sim \frac{T}{2\pi }\ln \frac{T}{2\pi }\)), the set of points \(\left\{ \frac{\rho _n}{k} \right\} _{k \in \mathbb {N}, n \in \mathbb {Z}}\) forms a dense subset of the imaginary axis \(i \mathbb {R}\).

By the Landau–Walfisz theorem  [ 12 ] , every neighborhood of every point on the imaginary line \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\) contains infinitely many branch points of \(P(s)\). Any attempt to analytically continue \(P(s)\) or \(\zeta _C(s)\) across \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\) encounters a non-isolated dense barrier of singularities. Therefore, the imaginary axis \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\) is a natural boundary for \(\zeta _C(s)\), precluding single-valued analytic continuation into the half-plane \(\operatorname {Re}(s) \le 0\).
