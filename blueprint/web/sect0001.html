0.1 Introduction

The arithmetic of the positive integers \(\mathbb {N}= \{ 1, 2, 3, \dots \} \) is governed by a fundamental duality between addition and multiplication. Multiplicatively, \(\mathbb {N}\) is generated freely as a commutative monoid by the set of prime numbers \(\mathbb {P}= \{ 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \dots \} \). Additively, \(\mathbb {N}\) is totally ordered, with each integer \(n\) generated by successive successor operations \(n \mapsto n + 1\).

The interplay between these two structures is captured by the Riemann zeta function, defined for complex arguments \(s = \sigma + i t\) with \(\sigma {\gt} 1\) by the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:zeta_def} \zeta (s) \equiv \sum _{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s}. \end{equation}
1

By Euler’s product formula, the multiplicative independence of the primes translates the Dirichlet series into an infinite product:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:euler_product} \zeta (s) = \prod _{p \in \mathbb {P}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^s}\right)^{-1} \quad (\sigma {\gt} 1). \end{equation}
2

The set of positive integers admits a canonical, mutually disjoint set-theoretic partition:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:fundamental_partition} \mathbb {N}= \{ 1\} \; \dot{\cup }\; \mathbb {P}\; \dot{\cup }\; \mathbb {C}, \end{equation}
3

where:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:composite_set_def} \mathbb {C}\equiv \{ n \in \mathbb {N}\mid n = a \cdot b \text{ for integers } a \ge 2, \; b \ge 2\} = \{ 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, \dots \} \end{equation}
4

denotes the set of non-prime composite numbers.

While classical analytic number theory primarily utilizes primes to study the zeros and distribution of \(\zeta (s)\), the structural properties of the complementary set \(\mathbb {C}\) contain deep, dual analytic information. Every composite number is an element of the infinite union of multiplication tables:

\begin{equation} \mathbb {C}= \bigcup _{k=2}^\infty \{ k \cdot m \mid m \ge 2\} . \end{equation}
5

From this geometric perspective, the composite numbers form a dense, overlapping lattice of arithmetic progressions, while the prime numbers \(\mathbb {P}\) appear as the topological vacancies or sieve holes left behind by the composite grid.

0.1.1 Central Thesis and Outline of the Monograph

The purpose of this monograph is to develop a complete structural theory of the additive and multiplicative Dirichlet systems generated by \(\mathbb {P}\) and \(\mathbb {C}\), and to map their analytic transitions across the complex plane.

The investigation is structured as follows:

  • In Section 2, we formalize the algebraic atoms \(\mathbb {P}\), establishing the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, modular sieve constraints (\(6k \pm 1\)), Fermat’s and Wilson’s theorems, and prove the Base-10 Radix Periodicity Theorem governing the cyclic decimal periods of \(1/p\).

  • In Section 3, we construct the algebraic taxonomy of the composite set \(\mathbb {C}\), partitioning it into pure prime powers and mixed composites via the von Mangoldt function \(\Lambda (n)\). We derive multiplicative identities for the divisor function \(d(n)\), the totient function \(\phi (n)\), and establish the Composite Base-10 Decomposition Theorem.

  • In Section 4, we construct three distinct Dirichlet generating functions for composites for \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\): the unweighted additive split \(\zeta _C(s) = \zeta (s) - 1 - P(s)\), the local prime-power Möbius bridge, and the divisor-weighted series \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s) = (\zeta (s)-1)^2\). We prove the Natural Boundary Theorem, contrasting the meromorphic continuation of \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s)\) with the \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\) natural boundary of \(\zeta _C(s)\).

  • In Section 5, we analyze the reciprocal Euler product \(1/\zeta (s) = \sum _{n=1}^\infty \mu (n) n^{-s}\), proving how square-free composites and primes establish exact parity cancellation, and prove the Mertens-Riemann equivalence connecting the growth bound \(M(x) = \mathcal{O}(x^{1/2+\epsilon })\) to the critical strip.

  • In Section 6, we execute a rigorous functional-analytic audit of the \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 1\) convergence boundary. We prove the three analytic obstructions preventing naive continuation of the additive completeness into the critical strip, derive the Dirichlet eta continuation, and analyze the phasor symmetry of the Approximate Functional Equation on the critical line \(\sigma = 1/2\).

  • In Section 7, we formulate the spectral bridge connecting Chebyshev’s \(\psi (x)\) to the complex zeros of \(\zeta (s)\) via Perron’s contour integration, deriving Riemann’s Explicit Formula and proving the Spectral Stability Theorem.

  • In Section 8, we synthesize all results into an overarching Arithmetic-Spectral Domain Matrix mapping arithmetic behavior across the complex plane.
