0.8.2 Final Epistemological Assessment

Our investigation yields three fundamental conclusions for analytic number theory:

  1. The Duality of Partitions: The additive partition of integers \(\mathbb {N}= \{ 1\} \; \dot{\cup }\; \mathbb {P}\; \dot{\cup }\; \mathbb {C}_{\mathrm{comp}}\) provides complete, exact closed forms for \(\zeta (s)\), \(P(s)\), and \(\zeta _C(s)\) in \(\operatorname {Re}(s) {\gt} 1\). However, this additive decomposition cannot be transported point-wise into the critical strip due to the divergence of coordinate sums (\(\infty - \infty \)) and the Riemann Series Rearrangement Theorem.

  2. Meromorphic Freedom vs. Natural Boundaries: The divisor-weighted composite series \(Z_{\mathrm{weighted}}(s) = (\zeta (s)-1)^2 = \sum _{c \in \mathbb {C}_{\mathrm{comp}}} \frac{d(c)-2}{c^s}\) algebraically eliminates all primes and the unit, extending meromorphically to the entire complex plane \(\mathbb {C}\) with only a double pole at \(s = 1\). In stark contrast, the unweighted composite zeta function \(\zeta _C(s) = \zeta (s) - 1 - P(s)\) inherits an impenetrable natural boundary along the imaginary axis \(\operatorname {Re}(s) = 0\) generated by the infinite harmonic accumulation of branch points \(s = \rho /k\) and \(s = 1/k\).

  3. The Spectral Invariance of Primes: Prime numbers and pure prime-power composites are unified under the von Mangoldt measure \(\Lambda (n)\). Through Perron’s formula and residue calculus, the non-trivial zeros \(\rho \) of \(\zeta (s)\) emerge not as arbitrary roots, but as the exact Fourier harmonics of prime fluctuation. The Riemann Hypothesis is the necessary and sufficient condition ensuring that these arithmetic waves never exceed the square-root damping threshold \(\mathcal{O}(x^{1/2}\ln ^2 x)\), preserving the fundamental structural stability of the integer number system.
